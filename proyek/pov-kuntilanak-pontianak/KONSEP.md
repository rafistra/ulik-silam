Asumsi:

- "Kamu" tidak bernama; lembarnya `Pendayung`. Juru mudi tua bernama Wak Saleh (sisipan).
- Narasi menyebut "Syarif Abdurrahman", bukan "Sultan", karena gelar sultan baru disandang pada 31 Agustus 1778.
- Waktu meriam (malam sebelum tiba) adalah tafsir; tanggal pasti tidak disebut di narasi.
- Meriam tidak disebut meriam karbit; tradisi itu masuk kartu Ulikan, bukan cerita.
- Momen hening di adegan 11, tepat setelah dentuman.

Pendayung:

```
Pendayung, a young Malay river paddler of about twenty from the Mempawah coast of West Borneo, a follower in a founder's boat party in 1771. Lean, wiry build; warm brown sun-darkened skin; oval face with wary dark eyes under straight thick brows, a broad nose and full lips; short black hair mostly hidden. A faded indigo plain cotton destar headcloth tied simply around the head with a small knot at the back. A loose collarless undyed off-white cotton shirt with sleeves rolled to just below the elbow; a checked cotton sarong in dark red and brown squares, hitched up and tucked at the waist so it falls to the knees; barefoot. A thin plaited rattan cord bracelet on the right wrist; no other jewelry and no weapon. Carries himself watchful and tense, shoulders slightly hunched, always gripping something.
```

Wak Saleh:

```
Wak Saleh, a Malay river helmsman in his late fifties from the Mempawah coast of West Borneo, 1771. Thin, sinewy and slightly stooped; deep brown weathered skin; narrow face with creased hollow cheeks, hooded heavy-lidded eyes, sparse grey eyebrows, a thin grey moustache and a wisp of grey chin beard; lips stained red-brown from chewing betel; short grey hair. A plain dark brown cotton destar headcloth knotted at the side of the head. A faded dark brown collarless cotton shirt hanging open at the chest; a dark green and black checked cotton sarong hitched to the knee; barefoot. A small plain cloth pouch for betel tucked into the waist of the sarong; no jewelry. Sits low and still at the stern, both hands firm on the long steering oar, eyes always on the riverbank.
```

Kuntilanak:

```
Kuntilanak, a female spirit of Malay folklore in West Borneo, 1771, only ever glimpsed and never clearly seen. A tall, very thin woman's figure; pale grey-white skin; her face always completely hidden behind very long straight black hair that falls past her knees. A long, loose, plain off-white cotton garment, simple and old, its ragged hem drifting as if underwater. Long thin pale arms hanging at her sides; bare pale feet that never quite touch the ground; no jewelry. Utterly still, head slightly tilted, as if listening.
```
