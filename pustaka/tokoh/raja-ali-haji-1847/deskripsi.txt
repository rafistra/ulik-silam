Raja Ali Haji, 38, a Malay-Bugis aristocrat, Islamic scholar and court adviser of the Riau-Lingga Sultanate, Penyengat Island, 1847. Lean and slightly stooped, medium height, warm medium-brown skin, long narrow face with hollow cheeks and prominent cheekbones, heavy-lidded dark brown eyes with a calm downward gaze, thin arched brows, straight nose with a broad tip, wide thin-lipped mouth, cheeks and jaw clean-shaven, only a faint thin black moustache and a small short tuft of black beard on the point of the chin, no full beard. Large white cotton turban wound high into a rounded, softly creased dome. Ankle-length long-sleeved outer robe of smooth plain warm dark brown cotton, worn open down the whole front like a long coat, its collar folded flat at the sides of the neck, with no buttons; under it a fitted light tan cotton inner shirt with a narrow pale embroidered placket of small cloth buttons down the chest, no gold; a light tan cloth sash wrapped at the waist; below the sash a long plain white cotton cloth hanging from the waist to the knees over loose white cotton trousers. Barefoot. A long string of dark wooden prayer beads around the neck, hanging to the waist. Plain silver ring with a dark red carnelian on the left ring finger. No gold, no silk, no keris. Calm and still, shoulders slightly rounded, arms hanging loosely, head tipped a little forward when listening.
