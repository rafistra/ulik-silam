Character reference sheet of Utuh. Utuh, a young Banjar man of about 19 from Pelaihari, South Kalimantan, in 1859, a gold panner and the viewer's closest friend. Short and stocky, warm brown skin, round cheerful face, wide dark eyes, thick eyebrows, a gap between his front teeth, a thin wispy moustache, black hair. Head: a faded rust-red plain cotton headcloth (laung) knotted at the front with the two ends sticking up. Body: a sleeveless loose vest of faded dark blue cotton, open over a bare chest. Waist: a plain brown cotton sarong hitched and tied above the knees over short black cotton trousers. Barefoot. In Act 1 a shallow round wooden gold pan (dulang) is tucked at his hip; from Act 2 on he carries a short parang in a plain wooden sheath at his waist instead. Restless and expressive, quick to laugh and to fidget with his hands.
Full-body front, side and back views in a relaxed natural standing pose, one three-quarter face portrait laughing, and one detail panel of the rust-red headcloth knot, the wooden gold pan and the sheathed parang. Plain light background.
