Montir Bertato, a Javanese motorcycle mechanic about 22 years old in a kampung near a market in Yogyakarta, Central Java, in 1983. Lean, wiry build with warm brown skin; angular face with high cheekbones, deep-set dark brown eyes, thick straight brows, a short broad nose, thin lips, clean-shaven; thick black hair in an early-1980s cut covering the ears and the top of the collar at the back. A faded blue-black tattoo of a small swallow above a simple anchor on the outer right arm just above the elbow; a healed pale cut scar across the left thumb; engine-oil stains on the fingers. First outfit: a faded navy short-sleeved cotton T-shirt with the sleeves rolled up toward the shoulders, dark grey cotton work trousers, black rubber flip-flops. Second outfit: a faded brown long-sleeved cotton work shirt buttoned at the wrists, with the same trousers and flip-flops. No jewelry, no watch. Restless and watchful, shoulders slightly hunched.
