Soegondo Djojopoespito, a Javanese law student in his early twenties and chairman of the Second Youth Congress, Batavia, 1928. Medium height with a compact, upright build; light brown skin; a broad face with a square jaw, straight nose and full lips; calm dark eyes under thick straight brows; clean-shaven, no glasses. Short black hair neatly trimmed, mostly hidden under a plain black velvet peci. A white cotton jas tutup with a high stand-up collar, five small brass buttons down the front and two lower patch pockets, over long dark trousers and black leather shoes. No jewelry; often holds a few folded sheets of paper or a pencil. Stands squarely and moves deliberately, as if weighing every word before he speaks.
