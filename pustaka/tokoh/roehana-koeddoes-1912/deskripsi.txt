Roehana Koeddoes, a 27-year-old Minangkabau woman from Koto Gadang, West Sumatra, in 1912, teacher and newspaper editor. Slender and of medium height, warm light-brown skin, oval face with high cheekbones, calm dark brown eyes under straight thick brows, small rounded nose, full lips pressed in a thoughtful line. Black hair parted in the middle and gathered in a low bun, mostly hidden. A long plain off-white cotton selendang worn as a loose tengkuluk over the head and shoulders, framing the face, not pinned. A loose unadorned dark indigo cotton baju kurung, collarless with a small chest slit, long wide open sleeves, reaching just above the knees. An ankle-length wrapped kain of soft brown hand-drawn batik with small scattered flower-and-leaf sprigs on a cream ground. Bare feet indoors. A thin plain silver bangle on the left wrist. Carries herself upright and still, head slightly tilted when listening, restless fingers that fidget with a pencil or a needle.
