Penebang Tebu, a fictional Javanese sugarcane cutter of about 32 who works the fields of the Cepiring sugar mill in Kendal, on the north coast of Central Java, in June 1983. Lean, wiry build of medium height, deep sun-darkened brown skin, angular face with high cheekbones, dark brown narrow eyes with fine squint lines, straight black eyebrows, broad nose, a thin black moustache, short black hair cut close at the sides and flattened by his hat. A wide conical woven-bamboo caping hat with a cotton chin cord; a faded olive-khaki long-sleeved cotton work shirt with a pointed collar and two chest pockets, sleeves rolled to mid-forearm, over a worn off-white cotton singlet; a small faded red-and-white checked cotton towel draped around the neck; loose plain black cotton trousers cut just below the knee and held by a cloth cord; barefoot, with thick cracked soles. A heavy single-edged golok cane knife in a plain wooden sheath tied at the left hip with a cord. Calloused hands with small pale cane-leaf scratches, no jewelry. Quiet, watchful eyes and slightly stooped shoulders from long hours bent over the cane.
