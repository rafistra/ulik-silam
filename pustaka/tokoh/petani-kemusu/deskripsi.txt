Petani Kemusu, a Javanese rice farmer about 40 years old from a village in Kemusu, Boyolali, Central Java, in the late 1980s. Lean, wiry build with sun-darkened warm brown skin; square face with high cheekbones, deep-set dark brown eyes with crow's feet, thick straight brows, broad flat nose, thin lips, short black stubble; short black hair with a few grey strands, pushed back. Outdoors he wears a woven bamboo caping (a wide, shallow conical farmer's hat); a faded off-white short-sleeved cotton T-shirt, worn thin and stretched at the collar; knee-length dark brown cotton shorts; a faded brown-and-maroon checked cotton sarong slung over one shoulder or tied at the waist; barefoot in the fields, worn black rubber sandals at home. A cheap steel wristwatch with a scratched face on his left wrist; no other jewelry. Carries himself with quiet stubbornness: shoulders squared, chin slightly lowered, hands rarely still.
