Suami, a young Banjar villager of about twenty-two from a riverside kampung in South Kalimantan, around 1900, waiting through the night his wife gives birth to their first child. Lean, wiry build; warm brown sun-darkened skin; oval face with anxious dark eyes under straight thick brows, a broad nose, full lips and a faint thin moustache; short black hair. A small rounded cap woven from reddish-brown plant fibre (kupiah jangang). A plain long-sleeved shirt of coarse unbleached off-white cotton (baju taluk balanga) with a round neck, a short slightly stiff upright band at the collar and three small buttons at the chest, sleeves rolled to mid-forearm; a cotton sarong (tapih kaling) checked in large and small squares of dark brown and faded indigo, tied at the waist and falling to the shin; barefoot. A thin plaited rattan cord on the left wrist; no other jewelry and no weapon. Holds himself tense and watchful, shoulders hunched, always gripping something.
