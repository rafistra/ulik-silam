Amai Laway Ngau, a Kayan Uma' Lekan elder and spokesman from the Apau Kayan highlands of Borneo in the early 1960s, a man in his late forties. Lean, sinewy build of an upriver farmer and boatman; warm medium-brown skin; long face with high cheekbones; narrow deep-set dark eyes; straight thick brows; broad flat nose; thin firm lips; faint lines at the eye corners; straight black hair cut short with a few grey strands; no tattoos; earlobes slightly stretched with no earrings. He wears a faded ivory-white long-sleeved cotton shirt with the sleeves rolled to the elbow, plain dark brown knee-length cotton shorts, and goes barefoot. A parang machete in a plain wooden sheath bound with split rattan hangs on a cord at his left hip. He leans slightly forward when he speaks, open palms raised, and listens with his head tilted.
