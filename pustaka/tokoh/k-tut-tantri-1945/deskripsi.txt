K'tut Tantri: a Scottish-born white woman of about 47, born Muriel Stuart Walker, an English-language broadcaster for the Indonesian Republican fighters in Surabaya in 1945. Small and slight, noticeably short; fair skin tanned and lightly freckled by years in the tropics; a narrow face with a pointed chin, lively grey-green eyes, thin arched brows, a straight nose and thin lips, fine lines at the corners of the eyes. Hair dyed black, shoulder length, pinned back loosely at the nape with a few strands escaping, a faint coppery-red tint showing at the roots. She wears a plain short-sleeved cotton blouse in faded khaki with a small pointed collar and two buttoned breast pockets, tucked into a mid-calf A-line skirt of dark olive cotton drill, a narrow brown leather belt, and flat brown leather sandals. A slim brown leather wristwatch on her left wrist; no other jewelry, no make-up. Quick, restless hands; she leans forward when she speaks, chin raised, alert and defiant.
