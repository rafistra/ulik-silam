Teman Pengukir is a Kenyah man of about twenty-five from the Apo Kayan highlands of central Borneo, in 1916, a paddler and woodcarver in the same convoy. Slim, wiry build, warm light-brown skin, a narrow face with high cheekbones, deep-set thoughtful eyes and a small scar on the chin. His black hair is cut in a straight fringe, the sides and crown are shaved, and the hair at the back hangs loose to the nape. His stretched earlobes hold thin brass rings, and a small round wooden disc sits in the upper rim of each ear. A dark blue curling tattoo design marks each thigh below the waistcloth. He is bare-chested and wears a long waistcloth of faded red-brown bark cloth; barefoot. A white shell bracelet circles his right wrist, and a small curved carving knife is tucked into his rattan belt. Calm and patient, his fingers always turning something over.
