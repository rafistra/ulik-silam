Pendayung Kenyah is a Kenyah man of about twenty from the Apo Kayan highlands of central Borneo, in 1916, a paddler in a supply convoy. Lean, powerful build with broad shoulders and heavily muscled arms, warm light-brown skin, a broad face, narrow dark eyes, straight brows, a flat nose and full lips. His black hair is cut in a straight fringe across the forehead, the sides and crown are shaved, and the hair at the back of the head hangs loose to the nape. A narrow band of plaited rattan circles his brow. His earlobes are pierced and stretched into long loops, each holding a plain brass ring. A dark blue tattoo of a curling dog-motif design covers the outside of each forearm. He is bare-chested and wears a long waistcloth of plain dark blue cotton wrapped around the waist and between the legs, its front flap hanging to the knees; barefoot. A plain dark hardwood bangle circles each wrist, and a Kenyah sword in a plain wooden sheath hangs at his left hip from a rattan belt. Alert and tireless, always watching the river.
